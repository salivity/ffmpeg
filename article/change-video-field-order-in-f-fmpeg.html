Change Video Field Order in FFmpeg

Interlaced video relies on field dominance—either Top Field First (TFF) or Bottom Field First (BFF)—to ensure smooth temporal playback. If a video's field order is reversed, moving elements will exhibit an unnatural jitter or stuttering effect. This guide explains how to use FFmpeg's fieldorder video filter to convert the field order of your footage by physically shifting the scan lines, correcting playback issues without needing to fully deinterlace the video.

How the fieldorder Filter Works

The fieldorder filter transforms the field structure of interlaced video by shifting the image up or down by one scan line and updating the internal field flag. This operation effectively inverts the temporal priority between the two fields (converting TFF to BFF, or BFF to TFF).

Converting to Top Field First (TFF)

If your source video is Bottom Field First (or is displaying jitter caused by being treated as TFF when it should not be), you can convert it to Top Field First using the tff parameter:

ffmpeg -i input.mov -vf "fieldorder=tff" -c:v prores -c:a copy output.mov

For H.264 video, specify your video encoder and interlacing flags explicitly:

ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vf "fieldorder=tff" -c:v libx264 -flags +ilme+ildct -top 1 -c:a copy output.mp4

Converting to Bottom Field First (BFF)

To convert Top Field First footage to Bottom Field First (commonly required for standard-definition DV formats):

ffmpeg -i input.mov -vf "fieldorder=bff" -c:v prores -c:a copy output.mov

For an H.264 destination requiring BFF:

ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vf "fieldorder=bff" -c:v libx264 -flags +ilme+ildct -top 0 -c:a copy output.mp4

fieldorder vs. setfield

It is critical to distinguish between fieldorder and setfield:

  • fieldorder: Modifies the actual image data by moving scan lines vertically, converting the visual contents from one order to another. Use this when the video's fields were genuinely captured or rendered incorrectly relative to your target specification.
  • setfield: Only alters the metadata tag indicating field order without altering video lines. If the image data is already in the correct order but the container header is falsely reporting the wrong field dominance, use -vf "setfield=tff" or -vf "setfield=bff" instead.

Verifying Field Order with idet

To verify whether your change was successful and to inspect the interlace characteristics of the output, run the idet (interlace detect) filter:

ffmpeg -i output.mp4 -vf "idet" -frames:v 300 -f null -

The output summary in the terminal will display counts for TFF and BFF frames, confirming whether your video is predominantly parsed in the desired field dominance.
