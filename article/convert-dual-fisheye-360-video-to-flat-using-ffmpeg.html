Convert Dual Fisheye 360 Video to Flat Using FFmpeg

This article provides a direct guide on converting raw dual-lens fisheye 360-degree video into a standard flat (rectilinear) perspective using FFmpeg’s v360 filter. By defining the input projection, selecting a flat output, and adjusting the field of view and viewing angles, you can reframe your panoramic footage into a standard video suitable for standard screens without dedicated 360 players.

The Basic Conversion Command

Dual-lens 360 cameras capture two circular fisheye images side-by-side. To convert this layout into a standard, flat perspective, use the following FFmpeg command:

ffmpeg -i input_dual_fisheye.mp4 -vf "v360=dfisheye:flat:h_fov=100:v_fov=60:yaw=0:pitch=0:roll=0:w=1920:h=1080" -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -c:a copy output_flat.mp4

Key Parameter Explanations

The v360 filter relies on specific key-value pairs separated by colons:

  • Input Projection (dfisheye): The first parameter specifies the input format. dfisheye tells FFmpeg that the video consists of dual fisheye lenses side-by-side.
  • Output Projection (flat): The second parameter designates the target format. flat produces a rectilinear perspective (the standard perspective used by conventional video cameras).
  • Field of View (h_fov and v_fov): Sets the visible area in degrees.
    • h_fov=100: Sets the horizontal field of view to 100 degrees.
    • v_fov=60: Sets the vertical field of view to 60 degrees.
    • Note: Setting these values too high can cause stretching at the edges of the frame, while setting them too low creates a heavily zoomed-in effect.
  • Camera Orientation (yaw, pitch, roll): Controls the direction the "virtual camera" points.
    • yaw: Rotates the camera horizontally (-180 to 180). Use this to choose which lens or direction to face.
    • pitch: Tilts the camera up or down (-90 to 90).
    • roll: Rotates the camera around the view axis (-180 to 180) to level a tilted horizon.
  • Output Dimensions (w and h): Sets the resolution of the output frame (e.g., w=1920:h=1080 for standard 1080p).

Fine-Tuning Lens Coverage

Most dual-lens cameras have an individual lens coverage slightly greater than 180 degrees (often 190° to 200°) to allow for image stitching. If the default dfisheye mapping creates edge distortion or warping:

  • ih_fov and iv_fov: Define the input horizontal and vertical field of view per fisheye circle.
ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vf "v360=dfisheye:flat:ih_fov=195:iv_fov=195:h_fov=90:v_fov=60:w=1920:h=1080" -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -c:a copy output.mp4

Selecting the Target Lens

If your subject was recorded by the rear lens rather than the front lens, rotate the view by 180 degrees using the yaw parameter:

ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vf "v360=dfisheye:flat:yaw=180:h_fov=90:v_fov=60:w=1920:h=1080" -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -c:a copy output.mp4
