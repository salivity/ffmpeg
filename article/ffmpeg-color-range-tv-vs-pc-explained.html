FFmpeg Color Range: TV vs PC Explained

In FFmpeg, the tv and pc color range options dictate the numerical boundaries used to represent luminance (brightness) and chrominance (color) in digital video. The primary difference is that tv represents a limited color range standard in television broadcasting, while pc represents a full color range standard in computing and photography. Setting these options correctly in FFmpeg filters ensures that video playback preserves intended contrast levels without crushing shadows or washing out blacks.

What is tv (Limited Range)?

The tv option, also known as "limited range" or "broadcast range," restricts the 8-bit digital values used for video:

  • Luminance (Y): 16 to 235
  • Chrominance (U/V): 16 to 240

In this standard, a value of 16 represents reference black, and 235 represents reference white. The values below 16 (footroom) and above 235 (headroom) were historically reserved in broadcast standards to prevent signal clipping and accommodate analog overshoot. Most broadcast television standards, DVD/Blu-ray media, and streaming codecs (such as H.264/AVC and H.265/HEVC in typical profiles) default to limited range.

What is pc (Full Range)?

The pc option, also referred to as "full range," utilizes the entire numerical spectrum available in an 8-bit container:

  • Luminance (Y): 0 to 255
  • Chrominance (U/V): 0 to 255

In this mode, true black is mapped to 0, and true white is mapped to 255. This format is standard for computer monitors, RGB color spaces (such as sRGB), video game capture, and web graphics.

Key Differences

Feature tv (Limited Range) pc (Full Range)
8-bit Luma Range 16–235 0–255
8-bit Chroma Range 16–240 0–255
Primary Domain Television, Blu-ray, Broadcast Computer monitors, Web, Gaming
Headroom/Footroom Yes (allows out-of-bounds signals) No (hard limits at 0 and 255)
Standard Profiles Rec. 601, Rec. 709, Rec. 2020 JPEG, sRGB, PC graphics

The Impact of Mismatching Ranges

Applying the wrong range during processing causes severe color degradation:

  • Full range interpreted as Limited range: Blacks become grey (lifted to 16), and whites become dull light-grey (dropped to 235), resulting in a washed-out image with low contrast.
  • Limited range interpreted as Full range: Any data below 16 or above 235 is discarded, stretching the mid-tones. This causes "crushed blacks" (loss of detail in dark areas) and "clipped whites" (loss of detail in highlights).

Using tv and pc in FFmpeg

The values are typically applied using the scale filter to convert between ranges or explicitly tag the stream's metadata.

Converting from PC to TV Range

To compress a full-range input (such as a screen recording) into a standard broadcast-compatible format:

ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vf "scale=in_range=pc:out_range=tv" -c:v libx264 -color_range tv output.mp4

Converting from TV to PC Range

To expand limited-range video so that it displays with full contrast in RGB-native workflows:

ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vf "scale=in_range=tv:out_range=pc" -c:v libx264 -color_range pc output.mp4

In these commands, the scale filter handles the mathematical expansion or compression of pixel values, while the -color_range flag writes the appropriate metadata to the container so that media players interpret the output correctly.
