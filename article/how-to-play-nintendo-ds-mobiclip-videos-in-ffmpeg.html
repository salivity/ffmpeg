How to Play Nintendo DS Mobiclip Videos in FFmpeg

This guide explains how to decode, play, and convert vintage Nintendo DS video files encoded with the proprietary Mobiclip codec using modern releases of FFmpeg. With native support integrated directly into the FFmpeg codebase, you can now preserve, view, and transcode these legacy media files into modern formats like MP4 without relying on outdated proprietary software or emulation tools.

Prerequisites and Codec Verification

FFmpeg introduced native support for the Mobiclip video codec starting with FFmpeg 4.4. Before proceeding, ensure your installed version meets this requirement.

To verify that your FFmpeg build includes the Mobiclip decoder, run the following command in your terminal:

ffmpeg -decoders | grep -i mobiclip

If supported, the output will list:

 V....D mobiclip             MobiClip

Playing Nintendo DS Videos with ffplay

Nintendo DS video files typically use container extensions such as .mods, .moflex (more common on 3DS), or raw video dumps extracted directly from game ROMs.

To play the video directly using FFmpeg's built-in media player, execute:

ffplay input.mods

If the file plays without audio or fails to sync automatically, you may need to force the video stream to a specific framerate native to the original Nintendo DS title (typically 15, 24, 29.97, or 30 fps):

ffplay -vf "setpts=N/(30*TB)" input.mods

Converting Mobiclip Videos to Modern MP4

To preserve Nintendo DS video assets or make them viewable on modern devices, convert the file into an H.264/AAC MP4 container:

ffmpeg -i input.mods -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset slow -c:a aac -b:a 192k output.mp4
  • -i input.mods: Specifies your source file.
  • -c:v libx264: Encodes the video stream to standard H.264.
  • -crf 18: Sets a visually lossless quality rate for the small DS resolution.
  • -c:a aac: Converts the audio stream into standard AAC.

Handling Raw or Headerless Video Streams

Some Nintendo DS games store video as raw Mobiclip streams extracted without standard container headers. In these cases, FFmpeg cannot automatically detect the format. You must explicitly declare the decoder, dimensions, and frame rate:

ffmpeg -f mobiclip -r 30 -i input.raw -c:v libx264 -crf 18 output.mp4

If the audio is stored as a separate track (such as raw PCM or FastAudio ADPCM), map both files together:

ffmpeg -f mobiclip -r 30 -i video.raw -i audio.wav -c:v libx264 -c:a aac -shortest output.mp4
